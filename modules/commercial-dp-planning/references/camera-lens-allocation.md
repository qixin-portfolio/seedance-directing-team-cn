# Camera and Lens Allocation

## Decide camera role before assigning shots

Typical roles:

- **Narrative A-camera:** locked composition, performance coverage, slider, jib, precise zoom, macro, product.
- **Movement B-camera:** follow, whip, low move, short orbit, transition, rapid repositioning.
- **Insert camera:** screens, controls, water, food, hands, texture, plates.

One camera may cover several roles, but the plan must show when rebuilding is required.

## Focal-length method

Choose in this order:

1. Required camera position and available space
2. Facial perspective and hand distortion
3. Number of actors and blocking width
4. Background compression and product visibility
5. Movement clearance
6. Lens speed and focus tolerance

Typical full-frame starting points:

| Shot need | Starting range |
|---|---:|
| Tight interior establishing move | 18–28 mm |
| Two- or three-person relationship | 28–40 mm |
| Medium performance | 40–55 mm |
| Reaction close-up | 65–100 mm |
| Screen, dial, sweat, water, button | 90–120 mm macro |

These are starting points, not fixed rules.

## Compatibility checks

- Native mount or tested adapter
- Lens weight and center of gravity
- Gimbal payload after mount, motor, filter, hood, and cable
- Front diameter and matte-box clamp
- Zoom barrel extension and rebalance requirement
- Focus motor torque and gear position
- Image circle and sensor mode
- Minimum focus and macro working distance
- Stabilization or autofocus behavior
- Lens metadata and distortion needs

If any item is unverified, write `待现场装机测试`.
